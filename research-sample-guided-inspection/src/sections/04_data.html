17Dataset Investigation

The core experiment needs, ideally: (i) many images of patterned dies of one design, so that some can be references and others targets; (ii) pixel-level defect masks; (iii) wafer and die coordinates, to form same-wafer and cross-wafer splits; (iv) several defect classes, to hold some out as unknown; and (v) several designs, for cross-type tests. This section checks what public data actually offers. All counts were verified against the official record (through search results quoting it) unless the table says otherwise.

17.1 Carinthia SEM defect dataset

What it is. Zenodo record 10715190 (version v2, published 27 February 2024), credited to KAI GmbH, Infineon Technologies Dresden and Infineon Technologies Austria [[carinthia]]. It contains 4,591 SEM images of defects on one production layer of unstructured (unpatterned) semiconductor wafers, unevenly distributed over six defect classes, packaged as a single data.zip with a separate HTML description. Labels are image-level (a CSV mapping image path to label).

  • SEM availability: yes, openly downloadable from Zenodo.
  • Defect categories: six classes. The class names should be taken from the record's description file; they were not confirmed in this session.
  • Resolution and annotation format: image-level labels in CSV. Resolution not verified (a third-party listing mentions 480 × 480 px).
  • Class imbalance: the record states that the distribution is uneven; exact counts per class must be computed in the dataset audit (milestone 2).
  • Access and permissions: open download. The licence was not verified. Note also that Carinthia-S cites the concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.10696644, which differs from the v2 record; cite the version actually used.

17.2 Carinthia-S

What it is. Zenodo record 16895427 (v1, 20 August 2025), credited to KAI GmbH and the University of Sarajevo, Faculty of Electrical Engineering [[carinthia_s]]. It is described as an enhanced version of Carinthia with expert-validated binary segmentation masks for each of the 4,591 defect images.

  • Suitability for pixel-level localisation: high. It is the best public source of real SEM defect masks found in this review.
  • Annotation quality: masks are described as expert-validated. Inter-annotator agreement is not reported in the sources we could access. The audit should inspect a random sample, checking anti-aliased edges, empty masks and class consistency.
  • Key limitation: the wafers are unpatterned, so there is no repeated die layout and no die-to-die correspondence. Rotation tests on these images test texture robustness, not layout alignment. Whether a defect-free subset exists is not confirmed; if not, normal references must be built from defect-free regions of defect images (masked by the expert masks).
  • Access and DOI: open download via Zenodo; DOI via the record; licence not verified.

17.3 WM-811K

What it is. The wafer-map dataset of Wu, Jang and Chen [[wu2015]]: 811,457 wafer maps, of which 172,950 are labelled into nine classes: Center, Donut, Edge-Loc, Edge-Ring, Loc, Near-full, Random, Scratch and None. Each map is a grid of per-die values (no die, pass, fail).

  • Representation: categorical die-level grids, not images of die surfaces.
  • Limitations for microscopic die-image inspection: no die appearance, no pixel masks, no possibility of testing rotation of die images, alignment, localisation inside a die or reference matching of die structures. The labelled part is dominated by "None".
  • Use here: only for the optional wafer-level study (Stage 9, RQ7), such as few-shot or open-set recognition of spatial patterns, and as a realistic source of spatial cluster shapes for the synthetic wafer benchmark.

17.4 MVTec AD

What it is. The standard industrial anomaly-detection benchmark [[mvtec,mvtec_ijcv]]: 5,354 high-resolution colour images in 15 object and texture categories, with a defect-free training set, a test set with more than 70 defect types, and pixel-precise masks. Licence: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial).

  • Suitability: excellent for initial method development, and for checking the implementation by reproducing published PatchCore and AnomalyDINO numbers. The few-shot protocol (sampling K normal training images) is well established.
  • Domain difference: natural colour images of objects and textures, not SEM or optical die images; most categories are roughly aligned; defects are often larger relative to the image than typical die defects. MVTec AD 2 [[mvtec2]] adds lighting changes and very small defects and is useful for illumination robustness.

17.5 Additional datasets

Wafer Surface Defect (IEEE DataPort) [[wsd]]: 500 optical images, 680 × 680 px, each showing one individual die; it includes defect-free dies and four defect types (particle, scratch, stain, liquid residual), annotated with YOLO-format bounding boxes. It is the closest public match to the "normal reference dies → inspect other dies" setting, but it is small, has boxes rather than masks, and has no wafer coordinates. Per-class counts and the licence were not verified, and access may require an IEEE DataPort account.

MixedWM38 [[mixedwm38]]: about 38,015 wafer maps (52 × 52) with 38 patterns (1 normal, 8 single, 29 mixed). Wafer-level only; the count comes from one secondary source and should be confirmed.

VisA [[visa]]: 10,821 images (9,621 normal, 1,200 anomalous) in 12 subsets with pixel masks; four subsets are printed circuit boards. It is the best public proxy for patterned, repeated designs and is useful for testing registration on real repeated layouts. The licence statements conflict between sources.

MPDD [[mpdd]]: metal parts in varied positions and orientations, used by RegAD and GraphCore to evaluate pose variation. Counts not verified here.

Private semiconductor datasets. Most recent semiconductor papers (SEM-CLIP, IC-DefectNCD, imec ADI/AEI, LogicQA's SEM set) use private data [[semclip,zhao2025ncd,prasad2024,logicqa]]. Their results cannot be reproduced and serve only as context.

17.7 Can the core experiment be done with public data?

Honest answer: only partly

No public dataset found in this review combines patterned dies of one design, pixel masks, wafer/die coordinates and multiple designs. Specifically:

  • Few-shot detection and localisation can be tested on real data: MVTec AD (method check), Carinthia-S (SEM with masks; references from normal regions) and Wafer Surface Defect (die-level references; box-level localisation).
  • Unknown-defect detection can be tested on real data: leave-one-class-out on Carinthia-S (6 classes) and on Wafer Surface Defect (4 classes).
  • Rotation robustness can be tested on real images by applying controlled rotations, but layout alignment of real patterned dies can only be tested on VisA PCBs (proxy) and on synthetic dies.
  • Same-wafer vs cross-wafer splits cannot be formed on any public semiconductor image dataset, because none provides wafer IDs and die coordinates for die images. They are tested on synthetic wafers and, if possible, on partner data.
  • Cross-type transfer can be approximated across datasets (e.g. enrol on Wafer Surface Defect, test on Carinthia-S), with the caveat that modality also changes.

Synthetic patterned-wafer benchmark (T3) design

  1. Layouts: several die designs built from parametric primitives (pad rings, via arrays, line gratings, logic blocks, orientation marks), rendered with an SEM-like or optical-like appearance model (blur, edge brightening, shot-like noise). The toy generator in prototype/ is the starting point.
  2. Wafers: each design is placed on a die grid inside a wafer outline. Each wafer gets its own nuisance parameters (global brightness, contrast, focus blur, noise level, small layout bias) to create controlled wafer-to-wafer shift.
  3. Geometry: each die image gets a recorded transform (rotation in {0, 90, 180, 270} plus a residual of up to ±15°, or any angle in 0–360° for the sweep; translation; scale within ±10%).
  4. Defects: injected with recorded masks. Shapes are cut from Carinthia-S masks (real shape statistics) or drawn procedurally (bridge, open, missing via, particle, scratch, stain), with sizes from 1 px upwards so small-defect behaviour can be studied. Orientation-dependent defects (e.g. a correctly shaped line at a wrong angle) are included on purpose to test invariance.
  5. Spatial patterns: defective dies are placed randomly or in clusters (edge ring, line, local blob) inspired by WM-811K classes [[wu2015]].
  6. Realism checks: compare intensity histograms, noise power spectra and edge profiles with real SEM (Carinthia) and optical (Wafer Surface Defect) images. Report synthetic results only as synthetic.
