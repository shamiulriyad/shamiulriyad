An Intelligent Computer Vision Framework for Wafer Die Defect Detection, Localization, Classification, Rotation Robustness, and Potential Root-Cause Analysis

Researcher: [Researcher Name] Student ID: [Student ID] Supervisor: [Supervisor Name] Institution: [Department, University] Date: [Month Year]
In one sentence We will build and evaluate a die-to-wafer inspection framework that detects and classifies defective dies, localizes defects where annotations permit, tests robustness to rotation and viewing angle, and links wafer-level patterns to candidate causes as evidence-graded hypotheses, not verified diagnoses.

1Problem & Motivation

Yield problems in wafer fabrication first appear as defective dies and as spatial patterns of failures across the wafer (edge rings, scratches, local clusters). Manual review is slow and inconsistent, and automated methods often address only one part of the task: image-level defect classification or wafer-map pattern recognition [1–3]. Real data also vary in orientation, alignment and viewing geometry, and unseen defect types appear.

Research question: can one modular framework link die-level evidence to wafer-level patterns reliably under geometric variation, and how far can those patterns support root-cause investigation without over-claiming?

2Research Objectives

  1. O1Detect normal and defective dies.
  2. O2Classify recognizable defect categories; localize defect regions where annotations permit.
  3. O3Analyze wafer-level defect distributions and spatial patterns.
  4. O4Evaluate robustness to rotation, alignment errors and viewing-angle variation.
  5. O5Investigate potential root causes from defect patterns and, where available, process metadata.

5Novelty & Expected Contribution

  • Die-to-wafer integration: one framework linking die-level detection and localization to wafer-level spatial analytics.
  • Explicit robustness protocol: reproducible tests under controlled rotation, misalignment and viewing angle.
  • Open-set awareness: flag unseen defect types instead of forcing a label.
  • Evidence-graded root-cause output: every hypothesis states its level of support.

6Data & Scope

WM-811K [4]: 811,457 real-world wafer maps; a labeled subset covers patterns such as Center, Donut, Edge-Ring and Scratch. Candidate data for O3 and for rotation tests (O4).
Limitation: WM-811K has pass/fail maps, not microscope images. Surface-defect detection and localization (O1–O2) and physical root-cause analysis (O5) need additional annotated images and process metadata.

7Feasibility & Plan

  • Phase 1: review [1]; WM-811K baselines; robustness protocol.
  • Phase 2: alignment, die detection, localization on image data.
  • Phase 3: anomaly detection, spatial analytics, root-cause study.
  • Resources: public data; Python, OpenCV, PyTorch; GPU [to be confirmed].
  • Main risk: no image or process data. Fallback: O3–O4 on wafer maps.
